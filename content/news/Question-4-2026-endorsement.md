---
title: "Mass Forward endorses Question 4 for Election Day Voter Registration"
date: 2026-11-2
---

The Massachusetts Forward Party endorses Question 4 for Election Day Voter Registration, which would allow eligible voters to register and cast a ballot at their polling place on Election Day. Voters could also update their address, but not their political party affiliation.
Low voter turnout is a major problem, particularly in off-cycle elections, when participation in Massachusetts can range from <a class="scorecard-link" href="https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/elections/research-and-statistics/voter-turnout-statistics.htm">15–25%</a> in primaries to <a class="scorecard-link" href="https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/elections/research-and-statistics/voter-turnout-statistics.htm">50–60%</a> in general elections. We support reducing barriers to participation, provided reasonable safeguards protect election integrity. We believe Question 4 strikes that balance.
The <a class="scorecard-link" href="https://electionlab.mit.edu/research/same-day-registration">research is compelling:</a> Same-Day Registration (SDR) generally increases turnout, with several studies finding gains of <a class="scorecard-link" href="https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/714776">3–7 percentage points</a>. The effect is particularly strong among younger and low- and middle-income voters, who are more likely to experience frequent address changes. The 10-day registration deadline is an unnecessary barrier that can prevent eligible voters from participating. It's time to eliminate it and join the <a class="scorecard-link" href="https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/same-day-voter-registration">24 other states and Washington, DC</a> that have enacted some form of same-day registration.
Opponents raise two legitimate concerns: election integrity and added administrative burdens. Question 4 addresses the first by requiring proof of residence and an oath affirming eligibility, with provisional ballots available when documentation is insufficient. These safeguards provide a reasonable balance between protecting election integrity and expanding access to the ballot.
Additional work and costs for election officials must also be taken seriously. Massachusetts has reimbursed municipalities for certain mandated election expenses, as it did following the <a class="scorecard-link" href="https://www.mass.gov/news/massachusetts-communities-to-be-reimbursed-for-2016-mandated-early-voting-expenses">2016</a> and <a class="scorecard-link" href="https://www.mass.gov/news/massachusetts-communities-to-be-reimbursed-over-11-million-for-2018-mandated-early-voting-expenses">2018</a> early-voting elections. Experience in other states suggests SDR costs are often manageable, and can be partly offset by reduced post-election processing of provisional ballots. We should plan and fund implementation responsibly, rather than let administrative concerns preserve unnecessary barriers to voting.
Change can be hard. But in Massachusetts, we know how to do hard things. We passed early voting in 2014 and automatic voter registration in 2018—each a step toward greater enfranchisement. We urge voters to support Question 4 and give more Massachusetts residents a voice in the political process.
